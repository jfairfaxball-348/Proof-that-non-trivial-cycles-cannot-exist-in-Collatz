# RL109 target — post-basin global route re-audit and RL110 theorem selection

Date: 2026-08-26

## Purpose

RL100–RL108 substantially changed the strategic landscape after the RL99 global audit. The first-Farey branch acquired a raw-multiplicity scope repair and strong ownership structure, while a sequence of direct ordinary-`+1` content/basin candidates was either converted into exact theorems with limited scope or frozen behind precise barriers.

RL109 is therefore a **global route re-audit**, not another narrow local calculation and not a bulk cascade session.

## 1. Strategic baseline

Use RL99's closure tree and route tournament as the starting map, then update it with RL100–RL108.

Retain without re-auditing expensive historical certificates after the incoming verification gate:

- the closed primitive/full-`D` radius-3 local contradiction engine;
- Gate-A even-`k` and terminal-`k<=25` closures;
- the Gate-A full-phase/ownership architecture;
- the exact first-Farey branch sieve and frozen numerical restart state;
- Gate-B's still-open `D|Q`-sensitive ownership problem;
- RL79's generalized-increment discriminator;
- RL80's backward-basin saturation no-go and analytic LTE basin families.

## 2. Mandatory post-RL99 updates

The route map must explicitly absorb at least these later findings:

- RL100: reduced first-Farey slope does not force raw `g=1`; natural absolute maximum envelope weakens with multiplicity;
- RL101: raw-`g` normalized cylinder compression, physical maximum = least cylinder representative, primitive-boundary excursion;

- RL103: direct affine first-surplus cylinder content scales with generalized increment and supplies no extra denominator factor;
- RL104: direct prime-prefix unit-increment content exhausted without a global consumer;
- RL105: roof factor-5 ordinary relation does not by itself escape rotation transport;
- RL106: aligned long all-even block runs are excluded, but no basin equality follows;
- RL107: boundary odd-core collision classes stay too few to create a growing product population; dyadic roof equality remains unforced;
- RL108: proper-prefix extremality is automatic, alternate LTE depth is rigid, and the word's discrete-log address classifies rather than forces basin type; fresh bounded local census exhibits non-dyadic capped words.

## 3. Routes that must stay frozen unless a new hypothesis breaks the barrier

Do not restart any of the following merely with different notation:

- pure local grammar or radius-by-enumeration without a global owned bridge;
- raw coboundary / normalized rotation identities;
- bare first-surplus cylinder congruence or least-representative arguments;
- direct affine or prime-local prefix content lacking a new global consumer;
- set-theoretic dyadic/backward basin saturation;
- alternate LTE-comb depth at the same pure roof;
- odd-core collision population using only RL106's run bound;
- scale-homogeneous strip/packing arguments defeated by RL79;
- untriggered bulk extension of the saved coupled cascade.

A frozen route may be reopened only if RL109 identifies a genuinely new input that invalidates its recorded barrier.

## 4. Primary audit question

Identify the **smallest still-missing theorem** whose proof would materially reduce global RL scope, not merely improve one branch constant.

Rank at least the following four theorem classes.

### A. Global owned radius-3 bridge

Can every genuine RL object, or one mandatory owned local configuration inside it, be forced into the already-closed primitive/full-`D` radius-3 input contract?

Audit exactly which hypotheses of the radius-3 engine remain unforced and whether RL82–RL108 now supply any new bridge to them.

### B. Gate-A branch/multiplicity/Farey coverage

Can the first-surplus maximum machinery be lifted from the exact first-Farey/raw branch to all admissible Gate-A branches through a universal domination, finite complement, or new raw-multiplicity exclusion?

Do not confuse normalized cylinder compression with absolute domination.

### C. Gate-B `D|Q`-sensitive ordinary ownership

Can an invariant consume the full ordinary word numerator/divisibility in a way that fails on RL20 `D∤Q` fakes and on RL79 generalized increments?

Prefer a theorem with an explicit physical or integer ownership consequence over another normalized identity.

### D. Direct full-phase ordinary-`+1` bypass

Is there a non-homogeneous physical equality, valuation, or lattice theorem that bypasses the Gate split and is not already one of RL103–RL108's frozen basin/content variants?

## 5. Audit deliverables

RL109 must produce:

1. an updated closure tree / proof-state matrix;
2. a ranked route table with explicit success condition, current obstruction, and red-team status for every live route;
3. a list of routes newly retired, retained, or reopened, with reasons;
4. one **precise RL110 theorem target** stated with quantifiers and scope;
5. if productive, at least one concrete analytic lemma or exact bounded experiment that materially supports the RL110 ranking.

Do not choose RL110 merely because a route is familiar or easy to compute. Choose it by expected global leverage after the audit.

## 6. Mandatory red teams

Every recommended theorem must answer:

- **RL20:** where is full denominator/word ownership consumed, and why does a `D∤Q` fake fail?
- **RL79:** what is genuinely special to ordinary increment `s=1`?
- **RL81:** are all bounded/equality claims about actual physical states rather than quotient coordinates?
- **primitivity:** where is primitive-cycle ownership required?
- **raw scope:** are reduced slopes, raw multiplicities, and Gate branches kept distinct?
- **external input:** which statements depend on the inherited computational floor?
- **closure scope:** exactly what would be closed if the theorem succeeded?

## 7. Cascade rule

The saved exact cascade remains a verified fallback, not the default RL109 workload.

Resume it only if the re-audit identifies a structural theorem or geometric threshold that materially changes its global leverage. Otherwise preserve its frozen restart state unchanged.

## 8. Desired closeout

RL109 should close with one of:

- a newly proved structural bridge that directly becomes the RL110 attack;
- a sharply ranked live theorem with a materially improved statement and supporting lemma;
- or a defensible conclusion that all currently identified routes require genuinely new external mathematical input, with the exact missing input stated.

Do not close merely with a literature-style summary. The purpose is to select the next attack with the highest plausible closure leverage.
