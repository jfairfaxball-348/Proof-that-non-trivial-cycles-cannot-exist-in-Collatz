## Core inherited objects

`n=(J-1)/2`.

The two hazard roots are

`alpha_1=-1`, `alpha_0=-2/3`.

For a boundary word `w` of length `t`, weight `r`, and ordinary parity numerator `Q_w`, define

`rho_(w,alpha)=(2^t alpha-Q_w)/3^r`.

RL292 proves

`Beta(n)=sup_(t,w,alpha) [nu_2(n-rho_(w,alpha))-t]`,

and therefore

`{Beta>=R}=union_(t,w,alpha) {nu_2(n-rho_(w,alpha))>=R+t}`.

RL292 also proves that no finite maximum of affine 2-adic valuation forms can dominate `Beta`, even after the local mod-3 reachability filter.

A first failure of the checkpoint-8 all-depth candidate enters one explicit depth-labelled 2-adic resonance ball. At `d=1`, these resonances are exactly the two `Beta` hazard templates.

## Primary theorem target

Prove a static danger-tree separation statement strong enough to imply Bellman domination. Preferred form:

> For every genuinely fixed-seed reachable positive odd boundary origin `(n,H)` and every static hazard-tree node `(w,alpha)` of depth `t`, if
> `nu_2(n-rho_(w,alpha)) >= R+t`,
> then the genuine historical/ballot area already paid is at least `R` (with the exact normalization required by the Bellman checkpoint threshold).

Equivalent min-plus formulations are acceptable. The theorem must price deep danger-tree entry using genuine fixed-seed ancestry; a statement about arbitrary local boundary states is known to be false.

If a full theorem is too strong, first prove it on the tight `(2,3)` / checkpoint-8 branch, then determine exactly which additional front-door states require separate constants.
