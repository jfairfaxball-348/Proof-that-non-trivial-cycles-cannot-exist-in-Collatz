# RL80 target — infinite proved-blue dyadic funnel / certified basin obstruction

Date: 2026-08-24

## Purpose

Investigate whether the rigorously certified basin of `1`, and especially its unconditional closure under multiplication by powers of `2`, creates a global obstruction to a hypothetical RL/nontrivial cycle.

This route is deliberately outside the homogeneous word/phase coordinate systems diagnosed in RL79. RL79 proves that essentially every primitive above-resonance word has a positive integer generalized `T_s` cycle and that many homogeneous invariants do not distinguish `s=1`. Basin membership is special to the actual ordinary Collatz map (`s=1`) and is therefore a genuinely new consumer.

## Core elementary fact

If a positive integer `n` is rigorously proved to reach `1` under the Collatz map, then

`2^k n`

also reaches `1` for every `k>=0`, by `k` even halvings.

Define the rigorously certified basin subset

`B={n>0 : n is proved/certified to reach 1}`

and its dyadic saturation

`B_2={2^k n : n in B, k>=0}`.

Distinguish carefully:

- unconditional analytic dyadic closure;
- externally verified finite ranges;
- analytic infinite subsets of the basin;
- heuristic/experimental evidence, which is not proof.

## Primary research question

# Can the infinite dyadic saturation of the certified Collatz basin be turned into an unavoidable structural obstruction to RL?

Do not count mere infinitude, density, or numerical proximity as closure. A hypothetical finite cycle only needs to avoid the actual certified set unless RL structure forces an intersection.

## Required investigations

### 1. Exact odd-core formulation

Every `N>0` is uniquely `N=2^v m` with `m` odd. Determine exactly how `B_2` is characterized by certified odd cores and what every state/owned predecessor of a hypothetical RL object must avoid.

### 2. Modular/residue coverage

Study analytically the set

`{2^k n mod M : n in certified base sets, k>=0}`.

Seek a theorem that intersects genuine RL residue/ownership constraints. In particular test moduli built from powers of `2`, powers of `3`, `24`, and the full denominator `D=2^A-3^L` where mathematically meaningful.

Mere high density is insufficient.

### 3. Multiplicative interval / scale coverage

For certified finite base intervals `[1,X]`, characterize exactly the dyadic union

`union_(k>=0) 2^k[1,X]` at the integer level and its odd-core content.

Ask whether bounded multiplicative gaps, logarithmic bands, or additional RL-compatible constraints yield a forced intersection. Be skeptical: one certified number per band does not trap a finite cycle.

### 4. Backward/preimage enlargement

Pure dyadic rays may be too thin. Build the largest rigorously legal backward basin tree possible without assuming Collatz.

For a known-blue `y`, legal immediate predecessors include:

- `2y` always;
- `(2y-1)/3` only when it is a positive odd integer and the odd Collatz step from it is exactly `y`.

Iterate only with exact integrality/parity checks. Formalize the resulting certified blue forest.

### 5. RL ownership/full-phase interaction — mandatory

Use genuine inherited RL information. Ask whether RL64/RL75 full-phase ownership, rotations, affine images, canonical predecessors, or the minimum-state architecture forces some owned integer into `B_2` or the certified backward forest.

A route that merely says “there is a blue number nearby” is invalid.

### 6. Minimum-state interaction

If `R#` is the least state of a hypothetical positive cycle, every cycle state lies outside the certified basin. Analyze certified blue numbers below/above multiplicative scales around `R#` and seek a theorem involving actual forced RL states, not ambient density.

### 7. Accelerated odd map

Translate the certified-basin structure to the accelerated odd Collatz map where useful. Dyadic saturation disappears into odd-core certification; determine whether this reveals a cleaner unavoidable condition on the odd cycle sequence.

### 8. A genuine barrier theorem

Aim for one of:

- every genuine primitive RL object contains/generates a state in the certified basin;
- the complement of the certified basin cannot support the exact RL ownership/residue structure;
- every RL-compatible scale/rotation/predecessor family contains an unavoidable certified-blue member;
- or a stronger theorem discovered during the session.

## Mandatory red-team tests

Do **not** mistake any of the following for closure:

- infinitely many certified-blue numbers;
- positive density;
- a blue number near every sufficiently large number;
- experimental verification of most numbers;
- “every number has a blue even multiple” unless the base number is already independently certified;
- assuming the odd core of an arbitrary integer is blue;
- treating a finite verified range as an analytic infinite theorem;
- any argument that would also work unchanged for RL79's generalized `T_s` fake cycles.

The last test is new and important: the useful theorem must exploit an ordinary-Collatz (`s=1`) fact.
