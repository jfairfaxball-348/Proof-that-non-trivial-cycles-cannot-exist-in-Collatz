## 1. Multiscale generalization of RL184's zero-defect lifetime

For an ordinary p-gap with endpoint heights `(a,b)`, put `M=max(a,b)` and write the normalized gap as

`x_(i+p)-x_i = C/2^M`

with positive integer numerator `C`.

RL181's universal normalized-gap ceiling gives

`C/2^M < 293,591,818,782 < 2^39`.

Hence if `M<=k`,

`0<C<2^(k+39)`.

On a clean common-mechanical successor transition whose corrected defect is zero, RL184 gives

`2^d C' = 3C`, with `d>=1`.

Consider `n=k+38` consecutive such zero-defect transitions. Iterating,

`2^(d_0+...+d_(n-1)) C_n = 3^n C_0`.

The right side contains no factor of two beyond the factor already in `C_0`, so

`2^(d_0+...+d_(n-1)) | C_0`.

Because each `d_j>=1`, `2^n|C_0`. Combined with `0<C_0<2^(n+1)`, this forces

`C_0=2^n`.

It also forces every `d_j=1`; otherwise the required 2-adic divisor would exceed `C_0`. Thus

`C_n=3^n`,

which is odd. The inherited ordinary parity sensor then forces the terminal p-edge defect to be nonzero.

Therefore a clean corridor of the `n+1=k+39` p-edges at offsets `0,...,n` cannot be defect-free.

## 3. Why the stronger incidence still does not make a strict excursion

The K corridor is

`128,081,997,553 < K_i < 146,795,909,391`.

Its full width is `18,713,911,838`. Ordinary drift is `Delta K_i=f_i/3`.

For a macro statement that must force an escape regardless of where its block begins inside the allowed K corridor, the block must generate a one-sided corrected-flow displacement greater than

`3*18,713,911,838 = 56,141,735,514`.

Since `|f_i|<1` for every ordinary defect, fewer than `56,141,735,515` same-sign ordinary defects can never guarantee that robust arbitrary-boundary one-pass displacement.

This does **not** say every physical K excursion needs 56.1 billion defects. The physical `K_0` is fixed, a block may begin favorably, and multiple blocks may interact. Those possibilities are exactly why a boundary-state/renewal theorem could still succeed.

## 4. Shallow-density stress test

`2N-L >= 56,141,735,515`.

With `L=137,528,045,312`, this implies

`N >= 96,834,890,414`.

So the one-pass route would need more than 70.4% of the entire p-rank cycle inside the chosen shallow set.

## 6. Renewal interface

The surviving route is not “count still more defects.” RL187 already shows that first-defect ownership is exact and leakage-free, and it drives ordinary absolute flow above 443. RL230 shows that larger shallow cutoffs can also force hundreds of millions more distinct nonzero defects.

What remains unavailable is chronological coupling between *successive* owned blocks.

A valid successor must produce a theorem of the form

`controlled boundary -> owned block -> controlled return/boundary`

with enough sign or displacement information to prevent each block from compensating the previous one.

The inherited p-shift height-cycle threshold balance does not supply this automatically. It is a statement in p-rank order, whereas the owned defect blocks are chronological. Treating those orders as interchangeable would be a scope error.
