# RL158 — dense-resultant character ambiguity and distinguished-root companion

## 1. Scope

RL158 continues the corrected RL157 phase formulation. It does **not** exclude the physical singleton owner and does not close Gate A, Gate B, RL/nontrivial cycles, or Collatz.

Let

- `D=2^A-3^L`, `gcd(A,L)=1`;
- `Ap-uL=1`;
- `rho=2^u 3^(-p) (mod D)`;
- `P_h(T)=sum_(j<L) 2^(H-h_j) T^(Aj mod L)`.

RL157 proved the necessary condition

`D | Res(2T^L-1,P_h)`

when the distinguished physical root satisfies `P_h(rho)=0 (mod D)`.

## 2. Exact counterexample to converse / discrimination

Take

`(A,L)=(13,8)`, `D=2^13-3^8=1631=7*233`,

and the valid nonnegative excursion

`h=(0,0,1,1,0,0,0,0,0)`.

For the RL157 construction,

`P_h(T)=2+2T+T^2+2T^3+2T^4+2T^5+2T^6+T^7`.

The exact resultant is

`Res(2T^8-1,P_h)=1631=D`,

but the distinguished physical phase has

`p=5`, `u=8`, `rho=1377 (mod 1631)`,

and

`P_h(rho)=17 (mod 1631) != 0`.

Therefore bare full-modulus resultant divisibility is **not sufficient** to recover physical ownership, even inside the corrected dense positive valid grammar.

Classification: **exact finite counterexample / method barrier**.

## 3. Character-twist mechanism

The failure is not accidental. Modulo each prime factor of `D`, the binomial `2T^L-1` has multiple roots related by `L`-th roots of unity. A resultant detects the existence of a common root, not that the common root is the distinguished `rho`.

For the exact example:

- modulo `7`, the physical root is `rho=5`, but `P_h` meets `2T^8-1` at `T=2`; the twist is `2/5=6`, a nontrivial eighth root of unity;
- modulo `233`, the physical root is `rho=212`, but `P_h` meets `2T^8-1` at `T=19`; the twist is `19/212=221`, again a nontrivial eighth root of unity.

Thus the two prime factors of `D` can be supplied by **different nonphysical characters**. Their product makes `D` divide the integer resultant even though `P_h(rho)` is nonzero modulo `D`.

Classification: **exact arithmetic explanation of the barrier**.

## 4. Distinguished-root companion

The same phase definition gives a second exact relation. Since

`rho^L=1/2 (mod D)`

and, from `Ap-uL=1` together with `2^A=3^L (mod D)`,

`rho^A=1/3 (mod D)`,

we obtain

`rho^(A-L)=2/3 (mod D)`.

Hence the physical root satisfies both

`B_1(T)=2T^L-1=0`,

`B_2(T)=3T^(A-L)-2=0    (mod D)`.

Because `gcd(L,A-L)=1`, a root-of-unity twist preserving both equations must have order dividing both exponents and is therefore trivial over every field of characteristic coprime to `6D`. In the RL158 example, for each prime `q in {7,233}`, the unique simultaneous root of `B_1` and `B_2` is exactly `rho mod q`; the nonphysical resultant roots fail `B_2`.

Even more sharply, the primitive binomial resultant is

`|Res(2T^L-1, 3T^(A-L)-2)| = |2^A-3^L| = |D|`.

So `D` itself is the elimination determinant of the two equations that distinguish the physical phase.

Classification: **analytic identity + exact finite audit**.

## 5. Strategic consequence

RL157's one-binomial resultant remains a correct necessary condition but is demoted as a standalone ownership discriminator. The live arithmetic object is now the **joint distinguished-root ideal** generated by

`2T^L-1`, `3T^(A-L)-2`, and `P_h(T)`.

RL159 should determine whether this joint structure yields a nontrivial Smith-normal-form/subresultant/divisibility invariant stronger than simply restating `P_h(rho)=0 (mod D)`. A successful invariant must preserve distinguished-root information and survive the RL158 counterexample; a tautological re-encoding of the original affine recurrence should be frozen as a barrier rather than promoted.
