# RL111 target — owned sparse support selection

Date: 2026-08-26

## Setup

Let `w` be the primitive parity word of a hypothetical positive ordinary shortcut cycle, let `D=2^A-3^L>0`, and let `u,v` be an RL109 canonical closest rotation pair after a common zero-flow cut. Thus

`0 != S=Q_1(v)-Q_1(u)=sum_(j=1)^R epsilon_j 2^(a_j)3^(b_j)`,

`D|S`, and `R=R_*`. Work on the complement `R>=4`.

RL110 proves that unrestricted fixed-content diameter and termwise support bounds cannot show `|S|<D`: their joint baseline stays above `27D/8`.

## Primary theorem target

Prove a genuinely ordinary-owned restriction on the selected support or its physical endpoints which forces one of:

1. `0<|S|<D`;
2. `R<=3`, enabling the inherited closed small-radius engine;
3. another contradiction to unscaled `D|S`.

An acceptable intermediate theorem must be global and must give a strict quantitative improvement that beats the RL110 baseline on every `R>=4` object. It should state exactly which full-ownership input makes it fail for the RL20 fake.

## Allowed consumers

- full ordinary `D|Q_1(r)` content for actual rotations, before normalization;
- actual physical state ordering or a proved physical strip for selected rotations;
- a relation between zero-flow cut location and a least-root/final-return owned arc, only if proved;
- a new support-strip/packing or valuation theorem for selected monomials;
- RL101 two-sided excursion only under its stated raw-branch hypotheses.

## Mandatory red teams

1. RL20: the argument must fail on its exact `D∤Q` radius-four fake.
2. RL79: generalized increment gives only `D|sS`; do not cancel `s`.
3. RL81: every physical state must arise from actual full `D|Q` ownership.
4. Primitivity: preserve distinct states and nonzero `S`.
5. Raw/Farey: do not add a branch restriction under global scope.
6. External input: label a least-cycle floor if used; prefer none.
7. Closure: no branch or finite output is a global closure.

## Stop/pivot conditions

Freeze a candidate that reduces to `S=D(x_v-x_u)`, to a normalized coboundary, to a generalized-increment-scalable identity, or to the RL110 diameter/termwise bound. Do not resume the cascade or a generic radius ladder without a new global support-selection theorem.
