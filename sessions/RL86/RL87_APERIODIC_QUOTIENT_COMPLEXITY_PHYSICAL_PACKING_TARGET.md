## 2. Start from the exact lift, not from an auxiliary heuristic

For a height-one quotient state

`J=3A-B+1`,

RL81 proved that `J` is a difference coordinate with a free physical common mode.  Equal or distinct `J` values do not automatically imply equal or distinct `A` or `B` values.

Therefore first derive the exact relation between the sampled

`J_i`

and the genuine paired physical states

`(A_i,B_i)`

at the same sampled positions.

Audit whether the common mode can be bounded by the first-Farey physical maximum strongly enough to make the fiber of

`(A,B) -> J=3A-B+1`

A potentially useful elementary fact is that, with

any fixed `J` has only finitely many physical lifts.  The question is whether the resulting multiplicity bound is small enough, and ownership-sensitive enough, to consume `930,959` distinct `J` values rather than merely restate the enormous interval `[R,M]`.

## 3. Seek a packing bound that uses scale economically

RL76's old barrier was that pump depth could hide in arbitrarily large physical scale.  RL85 repaired that for closed returns by capping the genuine maximum.

RL87 must now avoid reintroducing the same free scale in the aperiodic case.

For every candidate inequality, ask:

1. does its upper side depend only on `M` or `M-R` in a way already controlled by RL85?
2. does its lower side grow with the number of spaced-distinct quotient states?
3. does the comparison remain nontrivial when `M` is as large as `3^h`?
4. does the argument use the fact that `A,B` are states of the **same primitive cycle**, not arbitrary positive lifts?

A bound of the form `D_J<=O(M)` is useless at this scale.  Seek logarithmic, valuation, denominator, phase-slot, or weighted-mass budgets that are much smaller than the raw count of integers below `M`.
