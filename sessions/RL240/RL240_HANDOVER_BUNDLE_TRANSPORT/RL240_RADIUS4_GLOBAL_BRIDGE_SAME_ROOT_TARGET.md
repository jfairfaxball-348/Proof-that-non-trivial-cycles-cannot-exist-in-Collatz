# RL240 — Radius-4 global bridge / same-root encounter target

## Mission

Attack only the global bridge to the already-proved and RL239-audited
Radius-4 local theorem.

Do **not** prove Radius 5 in RL240. Radius 5 is a documented fallback, not the
active route.

The target is to derive a legitimate RL-specific mechanism forcing a retained
primitive full-`D` Gate-B object to encounter an exact cyclic
adjacent-transposition-distance-4 pair satisfying the inherited Radius-4
theorem.

## Inherited facts

1. Radius-4 local theorem: proved and independently audited.
2. Radius-5 viability: moderate new project; no Radius-5 theorem.
3. Bare cyclic geometry has no monotone shell principle: primitive words can
   avoid radii 3 and 4, or 3 through 5.
4. The inherited Radius-3 bridge history identifies a same-root obstruction.
   In the one-excursion language:
   - `f(T)=3T^q-2`, `q=a-ell`;
   - `L(T)=2T^ell-1`;
   - `Res(f,L)=2^a-3^ell`;
   - `P(T)` is the full phase polynomial.
5. The naive absolute-resultant bound is false at the exact `(65,41)`
   countermodel. A common prime in two resultants can come from different
   roots of `f`. Any valid bridge must retain the selected **same root** or
   an equivalent physical return identity.

## Weakest legitimate success theorem

Prove:

> Every retained primitive RL configuration in the full-`D` Gate-B branch
> forces two distinct eligible full-`D` rotations satisfying the audited
> Radius-4 hypotheses and having exact cyclic adjacent-transposition distance
> 4.

Do not strengthen this unnecessarily.

An equivalent proof is acceptable if it shows that the full same-root phase
conditions force an exact area-4 return to which the Radius-4 theorem applies.

## Attack order

### 1. Pin the exact live objects and selected root

Recover only the load-bearing definitions required to state, in one common
coefficient ring/modulus:

- the retained full-`D` object;
- the eligible rotations/returns;
- the selected root `rho`;
- `f`, `L`, and the exact live phase polynomial/remainder `P`;
- prime-power multiplicity requirements.

Use the packaged RL47 provenance and inherited Radius-4 scope first. Open
historical material only if an exact definition is genuinely missing.

### 2. Translate Radius-4 avoidance into return/phase language

Define precisely what it means for the live object to avoid every eligible
exact-area-4 return.

Prefer rank-displacement / prefix-flow variables already used by the inherited
programme. Derive necessary local states or residues for avoidance.

Do not assume low displacement implies Radius 4; prove the compression.

### 3. Seek a same-root incompatibility

At the selected root `rho`, combine the exact conditions corresponding to

`f(rho)=0`, `L(rho)=0`, and `P(rho)=0`

in the correct modular/prime-power sense.

Preferred outputs, in order of strength:

- explicit Bezout/subresultant certificate;
- root-specific congruence;
- finite residue/return covering theorem;
- monotone packing/return invariant that forces exact area 4.

A gcd of unrelated resultants is not sufficient.

### 4. If finite reduction appears, make it genuinely exhaustive

A finite covering/residue/return reduction is welcome only after proving that
it contains every retained physical case. Separate structural supersets from
actual RL cases and label evidence correctly.

### 5. Apply Radius 4 only after the bridge is proved

Once an eligible exact-distance-4 pair is forced, invoke the audited Radius-4
local theorem.

Do not claim Gate B closure before the global encounter theorem is complete
and all scope hypotheses match.

- Larger local radius != automatic global leverage.
- Bare-word geometry cannot prove the bridge by itself.
- `(65,41)` kills the naive absolute-resultant strategy.
- Same prime in separate resultants != same root.
- A finite grammar/countermodel search must be tied to the physical RL branch.
- Do not revive Radius 5 during this session unless a genuine Radius-4
  dependency failure triggers a formally recorded route reconsideration.
- Preserve the frozen RL231–RL237 charging/recurrence programme; do not
  silently combine Gate-A claims into this Gate-B route.

## Checkpoint classifications

At meaningful checkpoints classify progress as one of:

- `R4_BRIDGE_PROVED` — exact encounter theorem proved with all hypotheses;
- `R4_BRIDGE_REDUCED` — bridge reduced to a smaller explicit lemma or
  exhaustive finite problem;
- `R4_BRIDGE_BARRIER` — a proposed mechanism is rigorously blocked, with the
  next legitimate route identified.

Only `R4_BRIDGE_PROVED` permits application of the Radius-4 local theorem to
the retained Gate-B branch.

Gate A and global non-trivial-cycle exclusion remain separate unless their
inherited logical implication is explicitly established from authoritative
state.
