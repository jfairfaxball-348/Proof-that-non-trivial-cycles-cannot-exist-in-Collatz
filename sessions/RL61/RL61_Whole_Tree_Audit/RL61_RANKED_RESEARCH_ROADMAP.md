# RL61 ranked research roadmap

## Ranking principle

The next programme should be chosen by **global leverage**, not by proximity to the most recent terminal-tail calculation. The frozen safe-CF survivor is tractable and now finite in K, but it is only a restricted Gate-A subtree.

The roadmap below therefore ranks global theorem opportunities above continuity with RL60.

## Rank 1 — Uniform Gate A via genuinely coupled full-phase invariants

### Exact theorem target

Prove, for every retained one-excursion/full-phase object,
\[
H\ge t+3,
\]
or derive a contradiction under the exact same retained hypotheses.

### Why it matters globally

This is the cleanest outstanding theorem inside the retained one-excursion architecture. Unlike the safe-CF survivor, a uniform proof removes an entire top-level obligation rather than one denominator slice.

### Known blockers

- RL48 proves that cap/room plus total-displacement **separable** rank relaxation cannot close the large-`z` regime by itself.
- RL50 shows the isolated synchronized height-one subsystem is exactly shortcut Collatz, so an argument that discards the phase/ownership restrictions risks becoming circularly global.
- Local mass improvements alone have repeatedly sharpened bounds without producing a uniform contradiction.

### Best first lemma / experiment

Build a **macroblock inequality** that compresses each synchronized height-one run to its endpoints while retaining at least one nonseparable full-phase datum. Candidate coupled data:

- defect energy `E`;
- height-one telescoping;
- zero-position telescoping;
- terminal power `2^K`;
- full-denominator phase/ownership.

The first experiment should test whether the compressed identity forces a lower bound on cumulative excess `H` that grows with `t`, rather than merely with the number of local zeros.

Red-team the candidate immediately against:

1. the RL47 separable-relaxation witnesses;
2. the RL20 radius-4-packed local countermodel;
3. any proper-factor/coboundary constructions preserved in the reference snapshots.

### Stop/pivot criterion

Pivot if the proposed invariant, after forgetting full-phase labels, becomes a statement about **arbitrary** shortcut-Collatz trajectories. Also pivot if an exact model satisfying all retained coupled constraints violates the proposed inequality.

## Rank 2 — Rebuild Gate B from genuinely global ownership/divisibility

### Exact theorem target

From every genuine retained RL object, prove either:

- existence of two primitive `D`-divisible rotations satisfying **all** exact radius-3 hypotheses; or
- a direct contradiction from the same global data.

### Why it matters globally

Radius 3 is already closed. A valid exhaustive bridge converts a substantial body of completed local mathematics into a global obstruction.

### Known blockers

- RL20 kills any route using local endpoint/final-return grammar alone.
- RL49 kills the direct RL48 half-period radius-3 identification because its distance is always even.
- Canonical block/coboundary identities can be exact yet tautological, so divisibility must do genuine work.

### Best first lemma / experiment

Return to the two RL20 routes that explicitly survived the countermodel audit:

1. **balanced-return weighted difference**;
2. **strict-excursion packing**.

For the first pass, add the strongest later full-phase/same-root ownership information from RL43–RL50. Ask whether a balanced cut produces a nonzero integer whose absolute value is `<D` but which full ownership forces to be divisible by `D`.

If that telescopes to zero identically, move immediately to strict-excursion packing rather than polishing a coboundary.

### Stop/pivot criterion

Reject any candidate bridge that also holds in the RL20 `D∤Q` radius-4 countermodel or that never invokes a hypothesis distinguishing genuine RL ownership from local grammar. Reject any claimed radius-3 invocation until exact distance, orientation, gcd, primitivity, and `D`-divisibility are all checked.

## Rank 3 — Finish the frozen safe-CF survivor as a finite-K laboratory

### Exact theorem target

For the fixed convergent
\[
q=45{,}446{,}975{,}257{,}190{,}057{,}863,
\]
eliminate every remaining
\[
K\in\{25,27,\ldots,129\}
\]
under the full inherited phase and terminal-tail hypotheses.

### Why it matters globally

It would close the safe-CF/Legendre-covered subtree completely and produce a clean case study of how much the terminal-tail invariants can achieve. It may also expose a reusable finite-K lemma for Rank 1.

It does **not** close Gate A by itself, which is why it ranks below the two global programmes.

### Known blockers

- The final height-one tail is Collatz-conjugate if stripped of the extra phase constraints.
- Repeated local mass bootstrapping can give ever larger internal `z` floors without addressing the exact phase compatibility.
- The external K bound makes K41/K43 threshold extension low leverage unless used as a diagnostic rather than as the main strategy.

### Best first lemma / experiment

Exploit the newly finite set of 53 odd K values with an **exact backward terminal grammar plus phase filter**:

1. generate the finite symbolic predecessor grammar leading to `2^K` / `(2^K-2)/3` terminal targets;
2. carry the full phase/zero-position congruences backward, not just the shortcut trajectory;
3. seek a K-uniform congruence or valuation contradiction across all `K mod m` classes.

The goal should be one lemma that excludes a congruence class of K values at once, not another isolated terminal-ancestor threshold.

### Stop/pivot criterion

Stop if the backward tree expands into an essentially unrestricted Collatz predecessor cone with no additional phase filter, or if several K classes survive with no shared arithmetic obstruction after a bounded symbolic depth.

## Rank 4 — Direct full-phase impossibility theorem

### Exact theorem target

Prove that no genuine retained one-excursion/full-phase RL object exists, using the exact coupled invariants directly, without separately proving `H>=t+3` or manufacturing a radius-3 pair.

### Why it matters globally

This is the highest-upside programme: a strong enough result could bypass both Gate A and Gate B. It is ranked fourth because it is less sharply localized and therefore has higher proof risk.

### Known blockers

- Several exact phase polynomials and block identities have turned out to be coboundaries or proper-factor-compatible.
- Sparse/local identities need a size/nonvanishing/divisibility mechanism to become contradictions.
- Any reduction to arbitrary shortcut dynamics is unacceptable.

### Best first lemma / experiment

Combine four exact structures in a single normalization:

- defect energy `E`;
- zero-position telescoping;
- terminal power `2^K`;
- full denominator `D` and ownership.

Seek a normalized integer invariant `W` with the contradiction template
\[
D\mid W,\qquad 0<|W|<D.
\]
The first experiment should be symbolic and exact on macroblocks, with automatic checks against known coboundary identities.

### Stop/pivot criterion

Pivot if `W` vanishes identically, factors through a known proper factor rather than full `D`, or survives in the RL20 non-RL countermodel without using ownership.

## Rank 5 — Arbitrary-radius global packing reset

### Exact theorem target

Use the RL19/RL20 global population and product identities to obtain a radius-independent contradiction or a theorem forcing a bounded local radius from **global** RL divisibility.

### Why it matters globally

This may recover the original strategic promise of radius 3 without committing to the failed half-period geometry. It also gives a fallback if both the Gate-A terminal inequality and direct Gate-B constructions stall.

### Known blockers

- The local bounded-radius implication is false without global divisibility.
- Earlier packing bounds were too coarse to force radius 3.
- Continued-fraction gates alone do not control all ownership geometry.

### Best first lemma / experiment

Re-derive the global weighted-population identity in a form that keeps the location of balanced cuts. Search for a dichotomy:

- either a short owned displacement exists (then feed it into the closed local case tree), or
- every displacement is long, forcing a global population/weight lower bound incompatible with the denominator scale.

### Stop/pivot criterion

Abandon any formulation whose extremizers include the known radius-4 local countermodel after the full RL divisibility is removed; that indicates the proposed inequality is still only local.

## Recommended next session choice

Start with **Rank 1: uniform Gate A via coupled full-phase invariants**.

Reason: it has the clearest exact theorem statement, eliminates a top-level obligation if successful, and the project already has several underexploited exact coupled invariants. Use the finite safe-CF survivor as a testbed, not as the definition of the theorem.

A productive first milestone would be a small document containing:

1. one exact macroblock normalization;
2. one candidate nonseparable invariant;
3. verification on all known countermodels/witnesses;
4. a proof or falsification of a first lower bound that scales with `t`.

Do **not** resume K41/K43 as the default next move. Further thresholds are mathematically valid but currently lower leverage than resolving the scope gap exposed by this audit.
