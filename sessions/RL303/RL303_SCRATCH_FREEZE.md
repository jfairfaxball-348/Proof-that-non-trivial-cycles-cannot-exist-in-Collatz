## A. Bounded dyadic precursor evidence

The generalized lift suggested sufficient precursor inequalities

`m_8(C_(D,0))<=D-2`,

`m_8(C_(D,1))<=2D-1`.

Bounded/exact small searches showed the L precursor route is already too expensive at the first nontrivial cases; the R precursor looked much better after small depth but was not promoted as an all-depth theorem. The final report keeps the dyadic lift but rejects precursor-entry ownership as the main proof route.

## B. Direct finite R-wall evidence

Finite searches suggested

`m_8(RW_D)=B_R(D)-(D-1)/2`

for several small odd D, but no all-depth construction was found. Treat this as evidence only.

## C. Quotient gateway exploration

Several isolated short rewrites were first found numerically, including

`RW_D01001100=RW_(D-2)00000001`

and

`RW_D1100100=L_(D-2)0000001`.

They were later subsumed by the four promoted parameter families through the universal quotient gateway. The isolated words are not separate load-bearing claims.

## D. Renewal analysis of the leading-P defect

A two-state P/inverse-P renewal model was derived, with alternating run-length cost imbalance. Finite first-deficit searches looked strongly checkpoint-8 owned through several generations. This was not promoted: the root `R3 011` sector is already owned earlier, and generic renewal ownership would risk re-encoding the P Bellman problem.

## E. Integer wall debt

The first-P-wall cut initially suggested amortising a normalized `H_a=(0,a)` debt against the wall induction reserve. The physical `W_(D,4)` / `W_(D,0)` counterfamily disproved this generic strategy and is promoted as a barrier.

## F. Higher lattice rebasing

Since `P=(2,6-c) o (0,c)`, choosing `c=6` gives a left factor `(2,0)=Z o Z`. No universal cheap checkpoint-8 access to the corresponding `t+6` lattice cells was found in the first exact search. This remains an optional algebraic idea, not a proof route.

## G. P/Q route

The exact physical swap

`(P o Q)01110=Q o P`

and the Q return family

`Q 0(10)^r110=Q`

were found at the end of the session. The central open question is whether a finite set of such macros forms a complete commutation/refactorisation grammar through the actual `Q^n o U` wall stack.
