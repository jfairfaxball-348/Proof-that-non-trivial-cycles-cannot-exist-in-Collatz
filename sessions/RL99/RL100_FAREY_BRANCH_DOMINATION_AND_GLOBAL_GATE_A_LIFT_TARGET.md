### RL100 target theorem

Prove a **Farey branch domination / coverage theorem** of one of the following sufficient forms:

**Form A — exhaustivity.** Every genuine full-phase Gate-A violation has `(p,q)=(p*,q*)`.

**Form B — monotone domination.** For every admissible `(p,q)` with `q>q*`, derive a physical maximum / synchronized-block / reset bound that is at least as restrictive, in the terminal Gate-A contradiction, as the exact first-Farey branch.

**Form C — finite complement.** Prove that every violation lies either in the first-Farey branch or in an explicitly finite list of larger Farey branches, with exact branch parameters sufficient for certified elimination.

Any of A, B or C is a genuine success. Form B is likely the most realistic theorem shape; RL100 must not assume Form A.

## 4. Candidate domination mechanisms

Prioritize these theorem shapes.

### 4.1 Error-versus-scale monotonicity

The first-Farey physical cap comes from a lower bound on the resonance defect `D=2^p-3^q`, equivalently from the positive logarithmic error `epsilon`.

Determine whether larger admissible Farey pairs necessarily produce a scale cap that improves fast enough relative to their possible terminal `k` range. A useful sufficient inequality is one that makes

`C(p,q) / k`

or the corresponding reset-capacity ratio maximal at `(p*,q*)`.

### 4.2 Local/global slope coupling

RL83 already distinguishes equal versus distinct reduced local/global slopes. Consume that dichotomy quantitatively. If the local first-surplus slope differs from the global slope, the product of denominators is forced large. Ask whether the RL81 terminal state or RL19/RL20 global packing turns that denominator product into a physical scale contradiction.

### 4.3 Cylinder-height / ownership domination

RL84/RL85 show that the exact first-Farey cylinder becomes physically tiny. Generalize the cylinder-height calculation to a general admissible `(p,q)` and ask whether larger `q` makes the owned representative proportionally smaller, not larger, after the terminal constraints are imposed.

### 4.4 Finite branch frontier

If uniform monotonicity fails, derive a rigorous cutoff that leaves only finitely many Farey/convergent intervals capable of violating the Gate-A capacity inequality. Then enumerate **intervals/branches**, not raw `k` values.

## 5. Exact success condition

RL100 succeeds only if it proves a statement with explicit universal quantifiers over the complementary first-surplus branches.

Acceptable endpoints include:

- “Every Gate-A violation has the exact first-Farey pair.”
- “For every admissible `q>=q*`, the branch contradiction functional is worst at `q=q*`; therefore the existing first-Farey analysis dominates all branches.”
- “Every violation belongs to one of these `N` explicitly listed Farey branches, with exact parameter bounds.”
- “All branches with `q>Q0` are analytically impossible, and the remaining finite branches are explicitly enumerated.”

A new theorem that merely increases the minimal possible `q` is **not** sufficient.
