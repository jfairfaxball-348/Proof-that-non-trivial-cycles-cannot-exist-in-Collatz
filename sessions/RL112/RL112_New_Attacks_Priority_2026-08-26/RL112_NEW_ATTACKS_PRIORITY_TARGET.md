# RL112 target — New Attacks Priority, before the RL113 tournament

## Objective

Create an auditable tournament slate of genuinely differentiated attacks on
the mixed-base bottleneck for hypothetical ordinary non-trivial cycles.  The
output is a set of falsifiable sub-problems and cheap red-team tests.  It is
not a proof search and it does not authorize reviving frozen routes unchanged.

## Ranked research slate

The order is a research priority based on direct contact with the live
mixed-base obstruction, distinctness from frozen routes, and a short
falsification path.  It is not a probability of solving Collatz.  Each item
must be demoted immediately if it reduces to a frozen route or fails the
stated first test.

1. **Owned-support inverse theorem.**  Seek an inverse/additive-combinatorics
   theorem: a signed sparse `D`-multiple with actual rotation ownership and
   near-maximal coefficient mass must have a structured support forbidden by
   primitivity.  Value: directly supplies the missing RL109 support selection.
   First claim: classify two-term and three-term near-extremizers modulo `D`.
2. **Two-base finite transducer with a synchronizing defect.**  Construct an
   automaton carrying binary position, ternary exponent, and `Q mod D` rather
   than parity alone.  Value: a synchronizing forbidden state could force
   `R<=3`.  First claim: prove a finite-state quotient is sound for one
   closest-pair swap and rejects the RL20 fake only through ownership.
3. **Dyadic–triadic Fourier discrepancy of owned supports.**  Adapt finite
   group Fourier analysis to show an owned support cannot concentrate at the
   high `2^a3^b` corner required by RL110’s obstruction.  Value: yields a
   physical-strip or coefficient-mass saving.  First claim: a nontrivial
   character bound for exact rotations, not arbitrary fixed-content words.
4. **Entropy-increment rigidity for cyclic words.**  Use entropy/inverse
   theorems for a word whose rotation orbit has too many nearly equal `Q`
   values.  Value: converts the closest-pair condition into a low-complexity
   word, then tests that structure against primitive ordinary ownership.
   First claim: entropy deficit from a fixed `R_*>=4` closest-pair event.
5. **Effective S-unit gap with word legality.**  Couple a linear-forms-in-
   logarithms gap for `2^A-3^L` to the *legal support coordinates* of `S`,
   rather than to the global slope alone.  Value: could make a finite terminal
   reduction.  First claim: an explicit lower gap for a three-monomial legal
   sparse multiple that is not merely an existing continued-fraction bound.
6. **p-adic transfer operator on the owned rotation fibre.**  Replace random
   parity by a transfer operator conditioned on `D|Q` and a rotation class.
   Value: a spectral gap on this fibre could force escape from the dangerous
   support cone.  First claim: define a finite-level operator whose stationary
   measure differs from the unconditioned parity model.
7. **Geometry-of-numbers transference for support exponents.**  Embed the
   exponent pairs `(a,b)` and the logarithmic defect in a lattice; seek a
   transference statement saying a short owned sparse relation forces a
   forbidden second relation.  Value: supplies an alternative to coefficient
   diameter bounds.  First claim: a dimension-three exact lattice lemma with
   the RL20 discriminator built in.
8. **Mahler/automatic-sequence obstruction.**  Treat rotation numerators as
   values of binary automatic series and test whether a `3`-multiplicative
   relation would force rationality/periodicity incompatible with primitivity.
   Value: attacks the base mismatch via functional equations, not a parity
   density model.  First claim: a correct functional equation for the rotation
   family that preserves full numerator ownership.
9. **Symbolic-dynamical specification failure.**  Prove that a primitive word
   satisfying the owned closest-pair congruence cannot concatenate its local
   swap blocks without creating an illegal return.  Value: could turn a local
   grammar into a global theorem only if the specification statement is new.
   First claim: an extendability theorem for the exact RL109 zero-flow cut.
10. **Modular-expander lifting along selected primes.**  Choose primes where
    orders of `2` and `3` are simultaneously large, and prove that legal
    selected supports expand modulo those primes while `D|S` forces collapse.
    Value: a finite set of primes could establish a closable residue
    obstruction.  First claim: a deterministic expansion inequality for
    *rotation-selected* supports, not all subsets.
11. **Certified counterexample-guided synthesis of a strip invariant.**  Use
    SAT/SMT only to discover a candidate invariant over bounded local states,
    then prove its inductive step symbolically.  Value: rapidly kills false
    physical-strip conjectures while keeping the final theorem analytic.
    First claim: a candidate that survives all primitive words through a
    declared bound and fails on RL20 for the right ownership reason.
12. **Non-homogeneous `+1` cocycle.**  Search for a cocycle retaining the
    absolute affine increment at a chosen physical state, so generalized
    scaling cannot erase it.  Value: directly addresses the RL79 barrier.
    First claim: an identity that is false for increment `s!=1` but true for
    ordinary rotations and does not normalize to `S/D`.

## Tournament protocol for RL113

For each slate item, record: precise theorem target; which registry result it
uses; how it contacts mixed bases; universality analysis; RL20 and RL79
outcomes; primitive-word computation range; and one short proof obligation.
An item is not advanced merely by its ranking.  RL113 may retain,
combine, or reject any item only after the same evidence is recorded.
