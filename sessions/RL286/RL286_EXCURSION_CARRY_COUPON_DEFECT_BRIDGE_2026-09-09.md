## 2. Exact whole-component carry bridge

Let `x,y` be the paired binary words of one genuine positive first-return excursion, from `d=1` back to `d=1`.

Define its shifted-shadow/Ferrers carry integer

`C_E = W_x-W_y`.

Then exactly

`boxed: C_E = 3^r D_E / Q_in`.

Since `Q_out/Q_in=2^L/3^r`, equivalently

`boxed: C_E / 2^L = D_E / Q_out`.

Thus the whole-component 2-adic carry object discovered in RL285 is not an independent scalar. After the natural excursion normalization it is exactly the RL280/RL281 coupon defect.

`EXCURSION_CARRY_EQUALS_NORMALIZED_COUPON_DEFECT_PROVED`.

## 3. Proof in zero-rank coordinates

Write `rho=2/3`. RL281 gives

`D_E/Q_in = sum_j 2^(j-1) rho^(u_j) (1-rho^(h_j))`.

Using

`1-rho^h = (1/3) sum_(q=0)^(h-1) rho^q`

and multiplying by `3^r` gives

`3^r D_E/Q_in
 = sum_j sum_(q=0)^(h_j-1)
   2^(j-1+u_j+q) 3^(r-u_j-q-1)`.

This is exactly the Ferrers-cell expansion of `W_x-W_y` for the excursion.

The bridge is therefore termwise, not merely an endpoint numerical coincidence.

## 4. Height-supported component cells

For the `j`-th zero define the cell exponents

`p=j-1+u_j+q`,  `0<=q<h_j`.

There are exactly

`sum_j h_j = h`

such cells.

Because a first-return excursion begins with its mandatory entry zero, `u_1=0`, so the exponent `p=0` occurs exactly once.

The first-return nesting inequality

`u_(j+1)-u_j <= h_j-1`

implies the successive exponent intervals overlap or touch. Starting from zero, their union never advances beyond the total number of cells already paid. Hence every exponent obeys

`boxed: 0<=p<=h-1`.

Therefore a height-`h` first-return component consists of exactly `h` carry/coupon cells, all supported in the first `h` binary places, with a unique lowest cell.

Classification:

`EXCURSION_HEIGHT_SUPPORTED_CELL_DECOMPOSITION_PROVED`.

## 5. State-free height-only normalized-carry budget is false

The locally cheapest one-zero excursion has

`x=0 1^h`,
`y=1^h 0`.

For this family,

`boxed: C_E = 3^h-2^h`.

Therefore

`boxed: C_E/2^h = (3/2)^h-1`,

which is unbounded as `h` grows.

So the tempting recursive statement

"each excursion of height h creates at most O(2^h) normalized carry with a universal state-free constant"

is false.

In particular RL286 does **not** obtain a component theorem by simply assigning one binary carry unit to each height unit.

Classification:

`STATE_FREE_COMPONENT_HEIGHT_CARRY_BUDGET_BARRIER_PROVED`.

## 6. Why this does not contradict the cell support theorem

Every individual carry cell is supported below binary place `h`, but many lower-place cells can add and propagate carries upward.

The one-zero identity is the sharp demonstration:

`3^h-2^h = sum_(p=0)^(h-1) 2^p 3^(h-p-1)`.

The cell support is bounded by height; the *arithmetic carry chain generated by their odd coefficients* is not.

Thus RL285's first-cell rigidity and RL286's whole-component bridge are compatible: the first nonzero bit is rigid, while the later odd quotient still carries state-dependent information.

## 7. Plain checkpoint valuation induction remains unavailable

RL282/RL283 already established that legal positive excursions can raise `nu_2(J)` by more than their own height. RL286 therefore does not revive a blockwise theorem of the form

`nu_2(J_out)-nu_2(J_in)<=h`.

The component bridge explains the failure structurally: the passed quotient is controlled by the excursion defect together with the incoming arithmetic state, not by height alone.

The required invariant must include a reserve/state term that survives arbitrary zero-height positive boundary retention.

## 8. Preferred reserve coordinates for the successor

The new bridge identifies three exact pieces that should be coupled rather than studied independently:

1. component defect/carry:
   `C_E/2^L = D_E/Q_out`;
2. one-zero arithmetic reserve:
   `h=nu_2(J_in+4)` on the cheap one-zero channel;
3. multi-zero pricing:
   RL281's positive-checkpoint `F=Q(J+3)` and full-mass inequalities.

A viable successor theorem should seek a reserve `R` attached to the boundary/checkpoint state so that:

- arbitrary zero-height positive boundary retention transports `R` exactly or monotonically;
- a genuine first-return excursion spends height plus available reserve to account for its passed carry quotient;
- the reserve cannot be replenished for free by neutral boundary loops;
- terminal `J=2^k` forces demand at least `k`.

This is a target, not a proved theorem.
