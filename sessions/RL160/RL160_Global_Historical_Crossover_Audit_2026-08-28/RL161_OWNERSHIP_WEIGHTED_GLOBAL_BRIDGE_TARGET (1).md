# RL161 — Ownership-to-Global Quantitative Bridge Target

## Primary target
Prove or refute an exact theorem that converts **singleton/full-phase ownership** of the physical Collatz orbit into a **quantitative global constraint** incompatible with a non-trivial cycle.

The intended shape is

\[
\text{physical ownership at a distinguished phase}
\Longrightarrow
\text{forced global mass / packing / signed burden}
\Longrightarrow
\text{contradiction with exact cycle closure}.
\]

## Priority attack order
1. **Weighted-difference / perimeter bridge.** Identify whether an owned phase forces a non-cancellable signed contribution or lower bound in the exact weighted global identity.
2. **Population / packing bridge.** Determine whether owned phases require disjoint or capacity-limited physical representatives whose total demand exceeds what a cycle can supply.
3. **Ordered macro bridge.** If the first two fail, preserve the chronological valuation word and test exact affine/divisibility closure without collapsing to counts.
4. **Signed-defect / positive-lift bridge.** Use positivity or sign only where the physical ownership hypothesis prevents generalized-increment fake families from satisfying the same relation.

## Exactness requirements
A promoted theorem must:
- state the ownership hypothesis precisely;
- identify the genuinely nonlocal invariant being used;
- prove the implication analytically or by a deterministic exact finite certificate;
- apply to every putative non-trivial cycle in the claimed class;
- separate proved facts, finite certificates, inherited facts, computational evidence, conjecture, and method barriers.

## Banned shortcut classes from RL160
Do not promote as a new bridge:
- fixed-radius coordinate stacking through the same `u mod 2^r` state;
- unordered valuation counts/multisets as a substitute for chronological closure data;
- same-distinguished-root elimination/SNF without a new independence theorem.

## Promotion gate
RL161 may close when it has either:
1. an exact quantitative ownership→global theorem that removes a real proof obligation; or
2. an exact obstruction theorem showing a major ownership/global bridge also collapses, together with a sharply specified next live route.

A finite-radius branch sieve alone is not enough for global closure.
