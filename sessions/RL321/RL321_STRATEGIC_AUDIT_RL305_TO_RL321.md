# RL321 strategic audit — RL305 intention versus RL306–RL321

Date: 2026-09-14
Status: CLOSEOUT PROGRAMME ASSESSMENT

## Question

Did the sixteen sessions after RL305 stay true to the strategic audit/pivot, and do their repeated `PARENT_DIFFICULTY_DELTA = EASIER` claims amount to genuine material progress toward excluding positive non-trivial cycles?

## Verdict

Yes in strategic discipline, and yes in material mathematical contraction — but the `EASIER` labels are local ordinal judgements and are not additive percentages of a proof. The programme is substantially better structured and several large freedoms have been removed, yet it is not “sixteen easier steps away from done”. One project-level missing resource remains: a genuinely ownership-sensitive all-scale consumer that converts the now highly structured owned interfaces into contradiction or a complete finite certificate.

The correct summary is:

**materially closer, materially narrower, not yet proof-near.**

## RL305 intention

RL305 was not merely a new local attack. Its strategic rules were:

1. stop treating the RL304 P/Q commutation grammar as automatic priority;
2. reduce Gate A to its minimal scalar dependencies rather than prove stronger pointwise ownership theorems unnecessarily;
3. test the normalized wall route only while it shows a well-founded contraction;
4. hard-pivot if an unbounded state/context parameter survives;
5. preserve frozen work losslessly;
6. prefer a parent-near route over ever-deeper local grammar.

The central diagnosis was that many historical “coverage / ownership / incidence / reachability” gaps were manifestations of one missing global physical consumer.

## Compliance by era

### RL306–RL308: direct execution of the RL305 stop rule

RL306 did exactly what RL305 prescribed. It tested the normalized wall programme, found a genuine unbounded left-context parameter with zero relative contraction, froze the P/Q continuation, and pivoted to fixed-source affine/divisibility shells. This is strong compliance, not drift.

RL307 then contracted actual Gate-A dependencies instead of reopening O1/O2/P8. RL308 made one further exact prefix cut, then froze the local scalar line for a requested global mechanics review.

### RL309: the audit intention was generalized, not abandoned

RL309 explicitly superseded “Gate A first” with a harder-first global architecture. It identified the project-level parent dependency as universal full-D ownership/coercivity/extraction and made Gate A/B downstream fallbacks. It also introduced the requirement that each future checkpoint say whether the parent problem became EASIER, LATERAL, or HARDER, with automatic priority lost after repeated non-improvement.

This changes the literal RL305 target but preserves its deeper intention: avoid sunk-cost continuation, attack the minimum missing global arrow, and pivot on exact barriers.

### RL310–RL313: global extraction programme with enforced pivots

RL310 obtained the canonical physical min-to-max split, finite multiplicity fibres over each reduced slope, extremal aspect-ratio control, and a genuine physical-to-transport bridge. It also froze multiple routes when they were only recurrence-linear or ownership-blind.

RL311 compressed the one-sided extremal sector to controlled multiplicity or a short genuinely owned balanced return. When a Gate-A fallback produced a quantified exact barrier, it pivoted back to the global route rather than grinding it.

RL312 supplied ownership-sensitive balanced-return consumers and an all-scale divisor/full-activity dichotomy.

It concluded that the principal missing arrow was still an exhaustive all-scale ownership-sensitive coercivity theorem, kept P/Q frozen, corrected an over-optimistic spacing interpretation, and selected the RL311 controlled branch only under a strict stop criterion.

## What “easier” now concretely means

The cumulative progress is best measured by removed freedoms, not by counting labels.

At RL305 the project still faced several competing descriptions of the same global ownership deficit: seven Gate-A scalar obligations, an unbounded P/Q grammar, physical/resonance alternatives, and no universal reduced balanced witness.

At RL321 the following freedoms have been removed or sharply localized:

- P/Q no longer has automatic priority; its unbounded-context failure is frozen and understood.
- Every `g>1` candidate has a genuine reduced balanced return; this no longer needs to be encountered by a separate global theorem.
- Each fixed reduced slope has finite raw multiplicity; raw multiplicity is no longer independently unbounded.
- In the conditional external regime the reduced denominator is beyond `49,547,666,543`, and the first survivor is an exact single convergent pair.
- The `g=2` first-survivor geometry is no longer an arbitrary parity word: it has exact ordered-row / dual-shadow factor structure.
- The late-row contact no longer contains an unbounded integer quotient: the carry is bounded by `2^35`.
- RL321 removes even the signed-residue ambiguity: the ordinary physical tail canonically selects `0<J<3^s` and a common quotient.
- In the negative canonical branch, the least physical state is uniquely determined by one bounded carry `n`; it is not an independent large parameter.
- Several tempting routes are now rigorously known not to help: tail residue alone, corner smallness alone, homogeneous generalized-increment invariants, recurrence-only geometry, and the invalid height-38 scope transfer.

These are genuine contractions of the counterexample parameter space and of the proof-search space.

## Why this does not compound to “almost done”

The parent-difficulty label compares a checkpoint with its immediate incoming obligation. It is not a metric and cannot be multiplied across sessions. Several sessions also contained LATERAL/HARDER checkpoints or whole-session audits, and many `EASIER` steps are nested refinements of the same surviving branch.

Most importantly, no session since RL305 has closed Gate A, Gate B, `g=1`, or the global non-trivial-cycle theorem. The modern global route has concentrated heavily on `g>1`, and the late RL316–RL321 sequence is especially concentrated on the `g=2` first external survivor.

The remaining gap is smaller in description but still theorem-sized:

- positive canonical `Z0>0` needs an ordinary-owned consumer;
- negative `Z0<0` has a bounded Beatty carry but still an unbounded support geometry, and a `2^35` carry interval is not by itself a licensed finite exclusion;
- the root-aligned `kappa=0` branch and the link from the three-path clock to the actual reverse crossing remain open;
- `g=1` remains separate;
- higher multiplicities are not globally excluded merely because every `g>1` has a reduced return.

So the programme is **closer in architecture and parameter control**, not yet close in the sense that only routine checking remains.

## Programme-level assessment

Relative to RL305, the research has stayed unusually faithful to the requested pivot discipline. It repeatedly froze locally attractive routes when exact barriers appeared, ran two later strategic audits, and moved toward ordinary ownership instead of counting local identities as closure.

The strongest evidence that the progress is substantive is not the number of `EASIER` labels. It is the sequence of irreversible reductions:

`global encounter uncertainty`
`-> universal reduced balanced return for g>1`
`-> exact g=2 full-factor/shadow dichotomy`
`-> exact first-survivor ordered/root alternatives`
`-> bounded carry + signed residue`
`-> canonical ordinary residual/common quotient`
`-> bounded-carry Beatty normal form in the negative late-row branch`.

That is a real reduction in the complexity of a hypothetical counterexample.

The strongest reason for caution is equally clear: the last arrow — turning this owned normal form into impossibility — is still absent. The programme should therefore continue only while RL322 obtains another parent-near consumer (branch removal, support-independent upper bound, or complete finite certificate). If it merely refines the same residues without consuming a degree of freedom, the RL305/RL309 discipline says to freeze and pivot again.
