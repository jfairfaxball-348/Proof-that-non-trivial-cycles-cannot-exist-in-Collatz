## Motivation and inherited no-go results

If `y` is exactly certified to reach `1`, every dyadic predecessor `2y,4y,8y,...` is also certified blue. Whenever a blue node `Y` satisfies the legal odd-predecessor condition, `(2Y-1)/3` is an exact certified-blue integer; that node has its own dyadic tower and further legal odd predecessors.

RL80/RL81 already investigated the broad blue-basin idea. Reconstruct only the minimum needed to inherit these locks correctly:

- pure dyadic saturation is cycle-intersection neutral;
- arbitrary backward closure of a blue seed set is also cycle-intersection neutral as a set-theoretic trap;
- large or logarithmically dense blue coverage is not enough;
- proximity to a blue integer does not imply equality with a physical cycle state;
- the RL80/RL81 auxiliary-coordinate basin transfer failed because there was no exact basin-preserving bridge to a genuine physical cycle integer.

Do **not** spend RL218 merely generating more blue numbers, proving more density, or repeating the old auxiliary-transfer route.

## Modern reason to revisit the route

The hypothetical e=16 RL/H21 object is no longer free to occupy arbitrary integers. RL217 leaves **139,581,280** arithmetic candidates across **45,045** prefixes. Each prefix still has

`y_0 = y_0^* + 3*2^58 k`

on a short exact finite `k` interval, with terminal-Hensel restrictions, and RL217 now adds an exact finite union of 2-adic congruence selectors on `k` coming from the phase-51 universal height automaton.

The central question is therefore **not** whether the certified-blue basin is dense in the positive integers. Ask instead:

> Does the recursively certified-blue backward tree exactly cover, intersect, or systematically eliminate the particular arithmetic lattice/residue family in which the surviving RL/H21 candidates are now forced to lie?

## Success criteria

A strong positive outcome is an exact theorem or finite certificate that does one or more of the following:

- removes surviving RL/H21 arithmetic candidates because they are exactly certified blue;
- deletes an entire `k` residue class, H21 prefix, state, mod18 class, eta class or terminal rank;

- reduces the blue-cover problem to a small finite set of explicitly uncovered residue classes.

A useful negative outcome is also acceptable:
