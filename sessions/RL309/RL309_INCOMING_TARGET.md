# RL309 — global mechanics review, proof-architecture reset, and roadmap rework

Date prepared: 2026-09-13
Status: PREPARED, NOT STARTED
Session type: PLANNING / GLOBAL LOGIC REVIEW ONLY

## Absolute top-level objective

Put the actual theorem back at the top of the project:

> Prove that a positive non-trivial Collatz cycle cannot exist.

RL309 must not begin from Gate A, Gate B, Bellman states, selectors, walls, checkpoints, residual states, or the current local authoritative frontier. Begin with a hypothetical positive non-trivial Collatz cycle and reconstruct, in plain mathematical language, every credible route from that object to a contradiction.

The purpose of RL309 is not to solve the global problem. It is to produce the plan under which subsequent research will operate.

The session after RL309 — not RL309 itself — is the first session permitted to execute the redesigned mathematical programme.

## Binding strategic pivot

The current Gate-A/Bellman/scalar programme is frozen intact, not rejected.

All theorem-grade mathematics, finite certificates, exact identities, barriers, counterexamples, scratch, and live residual obligations from RL308 and earlier sessions remain available as resumable technical resources.

However, no local frontier has automatic priority merely because it is sophisticated or currently active.

RL309 must perform a clean global mechanics review and architecture reset before any further mathematical attack.

## Required starting question

For a hypothetical positive non-trivial Collatz cycle, ask:

> What do we know must be true of such a cycle, and what genuinely different mechanisms could make those requirements impossible?

The redesigned roadmap must flow downward from the global contradiction, rather than upward from whichever local frontier happens to be active.

## Plain-language proof mechanics reconstruction

For every major proposed proof route, state plainly:

1. What does a hypothetical non-trivial cycle give us?
2. What object is constructed from it?
3. What theorem or invariant applies to that object?
4. What must be proved next?
5. If that missing statement were proved, would it:
   - directly exclude the cycle;
   - eliminate only one class of cycles;
   - establish only a necessary condition;
   - feed another theorem;
   - or merely improve a local bound?
6. What still remains between that result and the final contradiction?

Only after this reconstruction may legacy terminology be mapped back onto the clean architecture.

If terminology no longer helps, replace or retire it.

## Global-leverage audit

Audit the important proved and partially proved objects across the full research history, including recent work.

Do not produce merely a chronological lemma catalogue.

For each major object or theorem determine:

- exact statement;
- hypotheses;
- whether those hypotheses are known to hold for every hypothetical non-trivial cycle;
- what contradiction, restriction, or reduction it supplies;
- whether it is logically independent of other machinery;
- whether it is merely another coordinate system for an already-known obstruction;
- what exact missing bridge prevents global closure;
- whether that bridge appears realistically provable.

Preserve important mathematics even when it is demoted from the primary route.

## Candidate global proof architectures

Produce a small number of realistic end-to-end architectures, preferably 2–4.

For each, write the full chain

`hypothetical non-trivial cycle`
`-> necessary global structure`
`-> intermediate theorem(s)`
`-> specific contradiction`
`-> no non-trivial cycle`.

Every arrow must be marked as one of:

- `PROVED`;
- `EXACT FINITE CERTIFICATE`;
- `EXTERNAL INPUT`;
- `OPEN BUT WELL-FORMED`;
- `SPECULATIVE`;
- `KNOWN BARRIER`.

For each architecture, identify the smallest genuinely missing theorem or collection of theorems.

A route is not promising merely because substantial mathematics has accumulated around it. It is promising only if there is a credible path from its present frontier to the final contradiction.

## Gate-A / Gate-B logic re-audit

Resolve the A/B architecture from first principles.

State precisely:

- what proving the present Gate-A theorem independently establishes;
- what hypothetical objects remain afterward;
- what proving the present Gate-B theorem independently establishes;
- what hypothetical objects remain afterward;
- whether A and B are complementary cases, sequential obligations, alternative proof routes, or terminology whose meaning has drifted;
- whether either can reasonably be strengthened into a direct global contradiction;
- whether the A/B split should remain in the redesigned roadmap.

Do not inherit previous shorthand conclusions unquestioningly. If historical use is inconsistent, repair the architecture explicitly.

## Depth-trap audit

For every active or recently active route ask:

> If the current frontier were solved completely, exactly what new global statement would become true?

Then ask:

> How many substantial unproved arrows would still remain between that statement and exclusion of a non-trivial cycle?

A route should lose priority if solving its current frontier merely reveals another comparable recursive ownership, coverage, valuation, ancestry, or incidence problem.

Preserve such work as a frozen tool; do not grant it automatic continuation.

## Redesigned roadmap

RL309 must finish with a concise roadmap containing:

### A. One clear top-level proof objective

No project shorthand. State the actual theorem.

### B. A small set of candidate global architectures

Prefer roughly 2–4 serious routes.

### C. One primary route

Explain why it offers the best present ratio of:

- global leverage;
- theorem plausibility;
- reuse of proved work;
- finite distance to contradiction;
- falsifiability and early failure detection.

### D. Explicit fallback routes

Preserve strong bodies of work as resumable alternatives.

### E. One-page dependency graph

Show what is proved, what is missing, and how each missing item advances toward the final theorem.

### F. Stop/pivot criteria

For each route define evidence that it is becoming another depth trap.

### G. Session-level progress test

Every future handover must answer:

> Is the proposed next session actually moving us closer to proving that non-trivial Collatz cycles cannot exist, or is it merely moving deeper into a local formalism?

The answer must name the exact global dependency shortened by the proposed work.

If that cannot be stated clearly, the next session must not automatically continue the route.

3. a major-theorem/object leverage audit;
4. a Gate-A/Gate-B terminology and logic re-audit;
5. a dead/depth-trap/retain-as-tool classification;

## Session discipline

RL309 is a planning session only.

It must not:

- continue the current Bellman/fixed-residue/Gate-A attack;
- prove the remaining scalar obligations;
- start a new speculative mathematical route;
- attempt to answer the global mathematical problem during the review;
- begin executing the chosen route after selecting it.

It may inspect historical reports, theorem ledgers, certificates, and frozen scratch as needed to understand global proof mechanics.

It may repair a genuine proof-state inconsistency only if necessary to make the architecture review valid. Otherwise it must not do new mathematics.

At the end, freeze and audit the plan, prepare RL310, and stop.

## Guiding principle

Preserve everything, simplify the logic, recover sight of the goal, identify the shortest credible global routes, and make future technical work earn its place by demonstrably shortening the path to the final contradiction.

Desired architecture:

> simple global architecture, sophisticated local mathematics where genuinely necessary.

The global proof plan should be explainable in plain English even when individual lemmas require deep mathematics.
