# RL-14 — Complete `j=1, P2` strict-interior closure and a new `P3` equal-gap descent

Date: 2026-08-20

This note continues RL-12/RL-13.  It does **not** claim that radius 3, or RL itself, is closed.  Its main theorem closes one more complete coprime same-direction one-orbit branch, and its second theorem identifies a new exact descent mechanism in the remaining `P3` branch.

## 1. Setup inherited from RL-12/RL-13

In the coprime one-orbit same-direction branch, the strict `[1,1,1]` simplex has positive gaps

\[
x+y+z=N.
\]

For the `P2` family, `N=L` and the canonical congruence is

\[
1+2\sigma^x+4\sigma^{x+y}\equiv0\pmod D.
\]

For the `P3` family, `N=B=A-L` and

\[
4+6\sigma^x+9\sigma^{x+y}\equiv0\pmod D.
\]

Here

\[
D=2^A-3^L>0,
\]

and `gcd(A,L)=1`.

For `j=1`,

\[
r={A+L\over3},\qquad \tau:=\sigma^{-1},\qquad \tau^r\equiv 6\pmod D.
\]

The `P2` subcase occurs when `A>2L`; the `P3` subcase occurs when `A<2L`.

## 2. RL-L86 — cyclic minimax for the `j=1, P2` normalized resultant

Put

\[
s=x+y<L<r,
\]

and rewrite `P2` in the reciprocal variable:

\[
G(X)=X^s+2X^y+4.
\]

Modulo `D`, `\tau` is a common root of

\[
X^r-6,
\qquad
G(X).
\]

The resultant is nonzero because `X^r-6` is irreducible and `deg G=s<r`.

At the unique prime above `2` in the Eisenstein extension generated by a root of `X^r-6`, the three terms of `G` have normalized valuations

\[
s,\quad r+y,\quad 2r.
\]

Since `s<r`, the minimum is uniquely `s`.  Therefore

\[
v_2\!\left(\operatorname{Res}(X^r-6,G)\right)=s.
\]

Because `D` is odd,

\[
D\mid {\operatorname{Res}(X^r-6,G)\over2^s}.
\]

Root-of-unity orthogonality gives

\[
D\le 2^{-s}
\left(16+4\,6^{2y/r}+6^{2s/r}\right)^{r/2}.
\tag{RL14.1}
\]

### 2.1 Limiting density `A/L=2`

Normalize the gaps by `L` and write again `x+y+z=1`.  At the limiting density `A/L=2`, we have `r=L`, and the squared exponential base in (RL14.1) is

\[
C(x,y,z)
=4^{-(x+y)}\left(16+4\,6^{2y}+6^{2(x+y)}\right).
\]

Using `x+y=1-z`, this becomes

\[
C=F(z,y),
\]

where

\[
F(u,v)=4^{1+u}+4^u36^v+9^{1-u}.
\tag{RL14.2}
\]

The three cyclic presentations are

\[
F(z,y),\qquad F(x,z),\qquad F(y,x).
\]

We prove that at least one is always strictly below `16`.

Sort the gap magnitudes as

\[
0<x\le y\le z,
\qquad x+y+z=1.
\]

There are two cyclic orientations up to rotation.

### 2.2 Orientation I: `(x,y,z)`

Use the presentation `F(y,x)`.

If `y\le1/3`, then `x\le y`, so

\[
F(y,x)\le F(y,y).
\]

The function

\[
F(y,y)=4^{1+y}+144^y+9^{1-y}
\]

is convex, so its maximum on `[0,1/3]` is at an endpoint.  Those endpoint values are

\[
14,
\qquad
F(1/3,1/3)=15.9178357072\ldots<16.
\]

If `y\ge1/3`, then `y\le z` implies `x\le1-2y`, hence

\[
F(y,x)\le F(y,1-2y).
\]

Again this is a sum of positive exponentials and therefore convex on `[1/3,1/2]`.  Its endpoint values are

\[
F(1/3,1/3)=15.9178357072\ldots,
\qquad
F(1/2,0)=13.
\]

Thus Orientation I always has a cyclic presentation with `C<16`.

### 2.3 Orientation II: `(x,z,y)`

The useful presentations are `F(z,x)` and `F(x,y)`.

If `z\le1/2`, then

\[
x\le {1-z\over2},
\]

so

\[
F(z,x)\le F\!\left(z,{1-z\over2}\right).
\]

The right side is convex on `[1/3,1/2]`, and its endpoint values are

\[
15.9178357072\ldots,
\qquad
15.8989794856\ldots,
\]

both below `16`.

Now suppose `z>1/2` and, for contradiction, that both

\[
F(z,x)\ge16,
\qquad
F(x,y)\ge16.
\]

Because `x\le1/3`, convexity in `x` gives

\[
F(x,0.3)\le
\max\{F(0,0.3),F(1/3,0.3)\}
<16.
\]

Since `F` is strictly increasing in its second argument,

\[
y>0.3.
\]

Hence

\[
x+z<0.7.
\tag{RL14.3}
\]

If `z\ge0.7`, this is already impossible because `x>0`.

Thus `1/2<z<0.7`.  Again by convexity,

\[
F(z,0.7-z)<16
\]

throughout this interval; it suffices to check the two endpoint values

\[
F(1/2,0.2)=15.0953450\ldots,
\qquad
F(0.7,0)=15.1282612\ldots.
\]

Since `F(z,x)\ge16` and `F` is increasing in its second argument,

\[
x>0.7-z,
\]

contradicting (RL14.3).

Therefore Orientation II also has a cyclic presentation with `C<16`.

Hence:

> **RL-L86.** For every positive simplex point `x+y+z=1`, one cyclic `j=1, P2` resultant presentation satisfies
> \[
> C<16.
> \]

This is a fully analytic minimax theorem.

## 3. RL-L87 — the entire `j=1, P2, [1,1,1]` interior is impossible

Let

\[
R={A\over L}>2,
\qquad
q=R+1.
\]

For a fixed normalized cyclic presentation, write

\[
p={6y\log6\over q},
\qquad
w={6(x+y)\log6\over q},
\]

and

\[
S=16+4e^p+e^w.
\]

Define the squared logarithmic margin per unit `L` by

\[
M(R)=
2R\log2+2(x+y)\log2-{R+1\over3}\log S.
\tag{RL14.4}
\]

At `R=2`, RL-L86 gives a cyclic choice with

\[
M(2)>0.
\]

Differentiate.  A direct calculation gives

\[
M'(R)=
2\log2-{1\over3}\log S
+{1\over3}{4pe^p+we^w\over S}.
\]

Now set

\[
H(t)=\log{16+4e^{tp}+e^{tw}\over21}.
\]

`H` is convex and `H(0)=0`, so

\[
H(1)\le H'(1).
\]

Therefore

\[
M'(R)\ge
2\log2-{1\over3}\log21
={1\over3}\log{64\over21}
=:\delta>0.
\tag{RL14.5}
\]

In the `j=1` lattice,

\[
A+L=3r.
\]

Hence

\[
A-2L=(A+L)-3L
\]

is divisible by `3`.  Since the `P2` branch has `A>2L`,

\[
A-2L\ge3.
\tag{RL14.6}
\]

Combining `M(2)>0`, (RL14.5), and (RL14.6),

\[
LM(R)>
\delta(A-2L)
\ge3\delta
=\log{64\over21}.
\]

Thus the normalized resultant bound forces

\[
{D^2\over2^{2A}}<{21\over64},
\]

or

\[
{D\over2^A}<{\sqrt{21}\over8}.
\tag{RL14.7}
\]

But a strict interior has `L\ge3`, and (RL14.6) gives `A\ge2L+3`.  Hence

\[
{D\over2^A}
=1-{3^L\over2^A}
\ge1-{1\over8}\left({3\over4}\right)^L
\ge {485\over512}.
\tag{RL14.8}
\]

Finally

\[
\left({485\over512}\right)^2>{21\over64},
\]

contradicting (RL14.7).

Therefore:

> **RL-L87.** The complete coprime one-orbit same-direction `j=1, P2, [1,1,1]` strict-interior branch is impossible.

No LMN estimate and no finite tail computation are needed.

## 4. RL-L88 — exact singularity criterion for the remaining `j=1, P3` side resultant

Now enter the remaining `j=1, P3` strict interior, so

\[
B=A-L,
\qquad
x+y+z=B,
\qquad
A<2L.
\]

The reciprocal form is

\[
H(X)=4X^{x+y}+6X^y+9.
\tag{RL14.9}
\]

Independently of `j`,

\[
\tau^B=\left({3\over2}\right)^3={27\over8},
\]

so modulo `D`, `\tau` is also a common root of

\[
F_B(X)=8X^B-27,
\qquad
H(X).
\tag{RL14.10}
\]

When can the integer resultant of these two polynomials vanish identically?

Suppose a complex root `\alpha` is common.  From `F_B(\alpha)=0`, at the `2`-adic valuation

\[
v_2(\alpha)=-{3\over B},
\]

and at the `3`-adic valuation

\[
v_3(\alpha)={3\over B}.
\]

The three terms of `H(\alpha)` have `B`-scaled `2`-adic valuations

\[
3z-B,
\qquad
B-3y,
\qquad
0,
\tag{RL14.11}
\]

and `B`-scaled `3`-adic valuations

\[
3(x+y),
\qquad
B+3y,
\qquad
2B.
\tag{RL14.12}
\]

For a nonarchimedean sum to vanish, the minimum valuation must occur at least twice.

The tie possibilities from (RL14.11) are:

- `x= B/3` with `y>B/3>z`,
- `z=B/3` with `y\le B/3`,
- `y=B/3` with `z\ge B/3`.

The tie possibilities from (RL14.12) are the opposite inequalities:

- `x=B/3` with `y<B/3<z`,
- `z=B/3` with `y\ge B/3`,
- `y=B/3` with `z\le B/3`.

The only simultaneous possibility is

\[
x=y=z={B\over3}.
\]

Conversely, if `3|B` and `x=y=z=B/3`, then with `Y=X^{B/3}`,

\[
8Y^3-27=(2Y-3)(4Y^2+6Y+9),
\]

so the resultant is indeed zero.

Hence:

> **RL-L88.** 
> \[
> \operatorname{Res}(8X^B-27,\ 4X^{x+y}+6X^y+9)=0
> \]
> iff
> \[
> 3\mid B,
> \qquad
> x=y=z=B/3.
> \]

In the coprime `j=1` branch, `A+L\equiv0\pmod3` and `gcd(A,L)=1`, so `3\nmid B=A-L`.  Therefore the side resultant is automatically nonzero throughout the live `j=1, P3` strict interior.

This removes the most dangerous algebraic escape.

## 5. RL-L89 — exact `2,3` divisibility of the `P3` side resultant

Let

\[
R_{B;x,y}=\operatorname{Res}(8X^B-27,H).
\]

The same local valuation calculation gives the guaranteed divisibilities

\[
v_2(R_{B;x,y})\ge
\min\{2B,\ B+3x,\ 3(x+y)\},
\tag{RL14.13}
\]

and

\[
v_3(R_{B;x,y})\ge
\min\{3(x+y),\ B+3y,\ 2B\}.
\tag{RL14.14}
\]

Because `gcd(D,6)=1`, these powers of `2` and `3` may be removed before comparing the resultant with `D`.

This normalization is cyclically compatible with the three equivalent `P3` presentations.

## 6. RL-L90 — equal-gap `P3` descent to a shorter binomial/cubic resultant

The factorization

\[
K(Y):=4Y^2+6Y+9,
\qquad
(2Y-3)K(Y)=8Y^3-27
\tag{RL14.15}
\]

produces an exact descent whenever two simplex gaps are equal.

### 6.1 Third gap larger

Suppose, after cyclic rotation,

\[
(x,y,z)=(q,q,q+d),
\qquad d\ge1.
\]

Then

\[
B=3q+d,
\qquad
H(X)=K(X^q).
\]

At any root `\beta` of `H`, (RL14.15) gives

\[
8\beta^{3q}=27,
\]

hence

\[
F_B(\beta)=27(\beta^d-1).
\]

A direct resultant calculation yields

\[
\operatorname{Res}(F_B,H)
=6^{6q}\operatorname{Res}(K(X^q),X^d-1).
\tag{RL14.16}
\]

### 6.2 Third gap smaller

Suppose instead

\[
(x,y,z)=(q,q,q-d),
\qquad 1\le d<q.
\]

Then `B=3q-d`, and similarly

\[
\operatorname{Res}(F_B,H)
=6^{6q-2d}\operatorname{Res}(K(X^q),X^d-1).
\tag{RL14.17}
\]

Thus in either case the entire `6`-free content is the same smaller resultant.

Let

\[
g=\gcd(q,d),
\qquad
k={d\over g}.
\]

The map on `d`th roots of unity `\zeta\mapsto\zeta^q` has image the `k`th roots, each with multiplicity `g`.  Therefore

\[
\operatorname{Res}(K(X^q),X^d-1)=S_k^g,
\tag{RL14.18}
\]

where

\[
S_k=\operatorname{Res}(X^k-1,K(X)).
\]

The roots of `K` are

\[
{3\over2}\omega,
\qquad
{3\over2}\omega^2,
\]

with `\omega^3=1`, `\omega\ne1`.  Hence

\[
S_k=
\begin{cases}
(3^k-2^k)^2, & 3\mid k,\\[4pt]
9^k+6^k+4^k, & 3\nmid k.
\end{cases}
\tag{RL14.19}
\]

Since `D` is coprime to `6`, a `D`-divisible `P3` solution with two equal gaps must satisfy

\[
\boxed{D\mid S_k^g.}
\tag{RL14.20}
\]

This is an exact descent from the large side length `B` to the imbalance parameter `k=d/g`.

Also

\[
S_k\le19^k,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{D\le19^d}
\tag{RL14.21}
\]

for every such equal-gap survivor.

This is the first denominator barrier in the live `P3` interior whose exponential size is controlled by **distance from equilateral geometry**, rather than by `A,L,B` themselves.

## 7. RL-L91 — nearest-equilateral `P3` interiors are impossible

In the coprime `j=1, P3` branch, `3\nmid B`.  Therefore the nearest integer points to the equilateral simplex are exactly

\[
(q,q,q+1)
\]

when `B\equiv1\pmod3`, and

\[
(q,q,q-1)
\]

when `B\equiv2\pmod3` after choosing the equal larger pair.

In either case `d=1`, hence `g=k=1`.  From (RL14.19),

\[
S_1=4+6+9=19.
\]

Thus `D|19`.  Since `D>1`, this would force `D=19`.

But

\[
2^A-3^L=19
\]

has no positive solution: for `A\ge3`, reducing modulo `8` gives

\[
0-3^L\equiv3\pmod8,
\]

which is impossible whether `L` is odd or even.

Therefore:

> **RL-L91.** Every nearest-equilateral strict-interior `P3` simplex in the coprime `j=1` branch is impossible.

## 9. RL-L92 — a universal `j=1, P3` parameter inequality: `D>4^B`

The equal-gap descent becomes stronger if one first proves a parameter inequality that is independent of the simplex composition.

Recall

\[
B=A-L,
\qquad
A<2L.
\]

Put

\[
C=2L-A=L-B.
\]

Because `j=1` means `A+L\equiv0\pmod3`,

\[
C=2L-A=3L-(A+L)
\]

is a positive multiple of `3`, so

\[
C\ge3.
\tag{RL14.22}
\]

Assume for contradiction that

\[
D<4^B.
\tag{RL14.23}
\]

With

\[
\delta={D\over2^A}=1-e^{-\Lambda},
\qquad
\Lambda=A\log2-L\log3>0,
\]

(RL14.23) gives

\[
\delta<2^{2B-A}=2^{-C}\le{1\over8}.
\tag{RL14.24}
\]

Hence

\[
\Lambda=-\log(1-\delta)
< {\delta\over1-\delta}
< {8\over7}2^{-C}.
\tag{RL14.25}
\]

Also `\Lambda<1/7`.  But

\[
\Lambda=L\log(4/3)-C\log2,
\]

so for `L\ge14`,

\[
C>0.4L.
\tag{RL14.26}
\]

Indeed the verifier certifies

\[
14\bigl(\log(4/3)-0.4\log2\bigr)>1/7.
\]

Combining (RL14.25) and (RL14.26),

\[
\log\Lambda
<\log(8/7)-0.4L\log2
<0.134-0.277L.
\tag{RL14.27}
\]

Apply the same published Laurent–Mignotte–Nesterenko two-logarithm estimate inherited in RL-11/RL-13.  Since `A<2L`, the same safe estimate

\[
{A\over\log3}+{L\over\log2}<4L
\]

applies.  The included verifier checks that at `L=27000` both LMN branches are already incompatible with (RL14.27), and the gap only widens thereafter.  Therefore any counterexample to `D>4^B` must have

\[
L<27000.
\]

The exact finite certificate scans the least `A` with both

\[
2^A>3^L,
\qquad
A+L\equiv0\pmod3.
\]

Only this first congruence-compatible `A` can satisfy `D<4^B`: after one further `+3` step, the ratio `3^L/2^A` drops below `1/8`, while the new `C` is still at least `3`, giving

\[
{D\over4^B}>7.
\]

For every `L<27000`, the exact scan finds zero coprime `j=1, P3` parameter pairs satisfying `D<4^B`.

Hence:

> **RL-L92.** In every coprime `j=1, P3` parameter pair with `D>0`,
> \[
> \boxed{D>4^{A-L}=4^B.}
> \]

**Dependency:** the infinite tail uses the same external LMN estimate already inherited in RL-11/RL-13; the finite part is exact integer arithmetic.

## 10. RL-L93 — all equal-pair `P3` interiors with the third gap smaller are impossible

Take an equal-gap strict interior of the form

\[
(x,y,z)=(q,q,q-d),
\qquad 1\le d<q.
\]

Write

\[
g=\gcd(q,d),
\qquad
q=gh,
\qquad
d=gk,
\qquad
\gcd(h,k)=1.
\]

Since `d<q`,

\[
h>k,
\]

and therefore

\[
B=3q-d=g(3h-k)\ge g(2k+3).
\tag{RL14.28}
\]

RL-L90 gives

\[
D\mid S_k^g.
\]

For every `k>=1`,

\[
S_k<4^{2k+3}.
\tag{RL14.29}
\]

This is immediate from (RL14.19): if `3|k`, then

\[
S_k=(3^k-2^k)^2<9^k<4^{2k+3},
\]

while otherwise

\[
S_k=9^k+6^k+4^k<3\cdot9^k<4^{2k+3}.
\]

Using (RL14.28),

\[
D\le S_k^g
<4^{g(2k+3)}
\le4^B.
\]

This contradicts RL-L92.

Therefore:

> **RL-L93.** Every coprime `j=1, P3, [1,1,1]` strict interior with two equal gaps and the third gap smaller is impossible.

Together with RL-L91, the equal-pair `P3` target is now reduced to the genuinely skew family

\[
(q,q,q+d),
\qquad d\ge2,
\]

with the third gap larger.  RL-L90 still gives the exact descent `D|S_k^g` there; only the size comparison remains open in the very-skew regime.

## 11. RL-L94 — the larger-third equal-pair branch is closed through `d<=4q`

Now take

\[
(x,y,z)=(q,q,q+d),
\qquad d\ge1.
\]

Write again

\[
q=gh,
\qquad d=gk,
\qquad\gcd(h,k)=1.
\]

Then

\[
B=g(3h+k),
\]

and RL-L90 gives

\[
D\le S_k^g.
\]

If

\[
d\le4q,
\]

then `k\le4h`.  We claim

\[
S_k<4^{3h+k}.
\tag{RL14.30}
\]

For `3|k`, this follows from `S_k<9^k` and `9^4<4^7`, because `k\le4h` implies `3h+k\ge7k/4`.

For `3\nmid k`, use `S_k<3\cdot9^k`.  The cases `k=1,2,4` are checked directly (and `k=3` is in the previous case).  For `k\ge5`,

\[
(3\cdot9^k)^4<4^{7k}\le4^{4(3h+k)},
\]

with the first inequality already true at `k=5` and strengthening thereafter.

Thus

\[
D\le S_k^g<4^{g(3h+k)}=4^B,
\]

contradicting RL-L92.

Therefore:

> **RL-L94.** Every coprime `j=1, P3` equal-pair strict interior `(q,q,q+d)` with `d<=4q` is impossible.

Combining RL-L93 and RL-L94, an equal-pair `j=1, P3` survivor would have to satisfy

\[
\boxed{(x,y,z)=(q,q,q+d),\qquad d>4q.}
\]

Equivalently the exceptional third gap is larger than five times either repeated gap, so

\[
q<{B\over7}.
\]

This is now a genuinely thin, highly skew corner of the simplex.

## 12. RL-L95 — complete exclusion of **all** two-equal-gap `j=1, P3` interiors

RL-L90 has an even simpler cyclotomic interpretation.

Suppose two gaps are equal, and rotate so

\[
x=y=q.
\]

Then the reciprocal `P3` equation is

\[
K(\tau^q)=4\tau^{2q}+6\tau^q+9\equiv0\pmod D.
\]

Divide by `9` and define

\[
\omega={2\over3}\tau^q.
\]

Then

\[
\omega^2+\omega+1\equiv0\pmod D.
\tag{RL14.31}
\]

Because `3\nmid D`, the element `\omega-1` is a unit modulo `D`: any prime divisor common to `D` and `\omega-1` would make (RL14.31) reduce to `3\equiv0`.  Hence `\omega` is a genuine primitive cube root modulo every prime-power factor of `D`.

Now

\[
{2\over3}=\theta^{L-A}=\theta^{-B},
\qquad
\tau=\theta^3.
\]

Therefore

\[
\omega=\theta^{3q-B}.
\]

If the third gap is `q+d`, then `B=3q+d` and

\[
\omega=\theta^{-d}.
\]

If the third gap is `q-d`, then `B=3q-d` and

\[
\omega=\theta^d.
\]

Thus in either unequal equal-pair case,

\[
\theta^d
\]

is a primitive cube root modulo `D`.

Let

\[
e=\gcd(L,d),
\qquad
L=e\ell,
\qquad
d=ek,
\qquad\gcd(\ell,k)=1.
\]

Since `0<d<B`,

\[
1\le k<B.
\tag{RL14.32}
\]

Comparing the two equal powers `\theta^{Lk}=\theta^{d\ell}` gives

\[
2^k=(\theta^d)^\ell.
\tag{RL14.33}
\]

If `3|\ell`, then

\[
